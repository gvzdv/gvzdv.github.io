Letter

to the reader

p. 03

To the reader,

I came to software from the field. For twelve years I ran industrial projects in project logistics and specialized rigging: more than 650 of them, with budgets up to $2.3M and teams of up to 30 people across six locations. That work taught me to scope carefully, to speak plainly with everyone from engineers to executives, and to finish what I start.

In 2023 I started building with large language models full time. Since then I have shipped RAG systems, agentic workflows, MCP servers, and evaluation pipelines for enterprise clients, a university research lab, and a startup I co-founded. At Bitovi I now own the architecture and delivery of an AI application that 6,000 people use every day.

I still work the way a project manager does. I start from the problem, put a prototype in front of people early, and measure whether it worked. The pages that follow cover the divisions of that practice, the record of operations, and a few experiments.

Mike Gvozdev Montréal, November 2026

Divisions

four divisions

p. 06

The practice runs as four divisions. Most projects draw on all four at once.

applied ai

Production LLM applications: retrieval-augmented generation, agentic and multi-agent workflows, MCP servers and tools, structured outputs, guardrails, and the evaluation that shows whether any of it works.

CaseRAG chatbot and LLM-assisted messaging for 6,000 daily users
Equipment

Agents: LangGraph, LangChain, OpenAI Agents SDK, Claude Agent SDK, Claude Code, MCP. Models: fine-tuning with LoRA, QLoRA, and PEFT; quantization with GPTQ, AWQ, and INT8; inference on vLLM; few-shot and chain-of-thought prompting. Ops: evaluation pipelines, LiteLLM, Langfuse, Weights & Biases, embeddings and vector search.

engineering

Hands-on build in Python and TypeScript: FastAPI and Flask services, Node.js, REST APIs, SQL, containers, and CI/CD. I write the code I design, and I stay on it through production.

CaseInfrastructure upgrades that cut single-call latency 20–30×
Equipment

Languages: Python (FastAPI, Flask), TypeScript and JavaScript (Node.js), SQL (PostgreSQL, MySQL). ML: PyTorch, Transformers, FAISS, Qdrant, Pinecone. Delivery: Docker, Kubernetes, Terraform, GitHub Actions, CI/CD.

software architecture

Solution design that starts in discovery and holds up in production: how models, data pipelines, APIs, and frontends fit together, and where the latency and the cost go.

CaseML architecture for IMAGE, McGill's open-source accessibility platform, across frontend, orchestration, and hardware
Equipment

Design: solutions architecture, ML component integration, data ETL pipelines, user-safety guardrails, SQL and vector databases. Cloud: GCP (Cloud Run, Vertex AI), AWS (Bedrock, Lambda, EC2, S3, ECS), Azure.

product engineering

From a blank page to something people use: customer discovery, proof-of-concept and MVP builds, launches, and growth. Twelve years of client-facing delivery taught me to scope with the customer, not around them.

CaseA charity app scaled to 126K users in one week; co-founder and CTO at Ingsikt
Equipment

Delivery: customer discovery, PoC, MVP, and proof-of-value builds, presales-to-postsales ownership. Teams: Scrum and Kanban, stakeholder management, cross-functional coordination, mentorship.

claudish-to-english

2.7KGitHub stars
132forks

A Claude Code plugin that shows a plain-English rewrite of each assistant message. The rewrite comes from a local model through ollama by default, or from the codex CLI, the Anthropic API, or any OpenAI-compatible API. It only changes what you read: Claude's reasoning and the saved transcript keep the original, and every hook fails open, so a provider outage never hides an answer.
